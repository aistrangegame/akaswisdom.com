02 — What it is Two layers · one fabric · the engineered bond

Two materials
that should not
belong together.

Apolyurethane film cannot be sewn from. A polyester knit cannot hold water. Neither, alone, is the fabric you need. ProSoft PUL is the marriage: a 1-mil TPU film heat-bonded to a 155 GSM polyester interlock — fused under temperature and pressure into a single substrate. The film is the working layer: waterproof from above, vapor-permeable through. The knit is the body — what your needle finds, what your hand feels, what carries the load.

04 — Sewing The workshop · machine · needle · seal

Every stitch
is a hole
in the barrier.

The fabric arrives ready. No pre-washing, no conditioning. Cut it, sew it, ship it. But the moment your needle enters the film, you've made a permanent hole — the kind only heat can close. Working with PUL is working with that knowledge, every stitch. What follows is the setup that makes the hole as small as possible — and the seal that closes it after.

The workbench · five stations

Five decisions before the first stitch.

01 / 05 · in build order
Station 01 — Foot

Walking foot or Teflon — never standard.

A standard presser foot sticks to the polyurethane film. Drag, skip, uneven feed. The film is too smooth and too tacky for a regular foot. The fix is mechanical: a walking foot that grips the fabric in synchronized motion with the feed dogs, or a Teflon (glide) foot that slides over the film without adhesion.

Two field workarounds when neither foot is available: sew through wax paper laid over the film and tear it away after, or dab a thin film of machine oil on the underside of a standard foot.

"Walking foot or Teflon/glide foot is essential — standard feet stick to the film, causing drag. Alternatives: sew through wax paper (tear away after), or dab machine oil on the foot."

Station 02 — Needle

Ballpoint, not sharp.

Sharp needles slice the polyester yarns of the interlock base, enlarging the hole on every pass. Ballpoint or jersey needles push between yarns instead of through them, leaving the smallest possible puncture in the film above. The hole is unavoidable. The size of it is in your needle choice.

Sizes 75/11 or 80/12 — calibrated for the 1-mil film and 155 GSM knit beneath.

"Use ballpoint/jersey needles (sizes 75/11 or 80/12) — the interlock knit base requires ballpoint tips that push between yarns. Sharp needles slice yarns and enlarge needle holes, creating leak points."

Station 03 — Thread

Polyester only. Not cotton. Not rayon.

Cotton and rayon thread are hygroscopic — they absorb moisture and wick water through the very needle holes you've worked to keep small. Quality polyester (Tex-27 or 65/2 grade) does not wick. The thread holds its place in the puncture without becoming the leak path itself.

The choice of thread is part of the seal. The seam tape comes after.

"Quality polyester thread (Tex-27 or 65/2 grade) exclusively. Cotton and rayon wick moisture through needle holes, defeating the barrier."

Station 04 — Orientation

Film side down.

The feed dogs need grip. The film surface is too slick for them to advance reliably; the textile face is what they bite into. Film side down against the feed dogs, textile face up under the foot. The teeth grip the knit; the foot glides over the film.

A small choice. It decides whether your seam line stays straight.

"Film side down against feed dogs — teeth grip the slippery surface while the textile face rides smoothly under the foot."

Station 05 — Cutting

Clips, not pins.

Pins puncture the film before the needle ever does. Every pinhole is a permanent hole — same physics as the needle, only made before construction even begins. Use clips instead of pins. Mark on the textile side only; permanent marker shows up cleanly without bleeding through.

The fabric cuts cleanly without fraying. No edge finishing required for the cut itself.

"Cuts cleanly without fraying — no edge finishing required. Use clips instead of pins. Mark on the textile side only."

— The seal · heat-activated

The seal.

Heat-activated seam sealing tape, applied with a warm iron or heat applicator, melts into the needle holes and restores the waterproof barrier. The tape is a sealant — not a structural replacement for stitching. The stitching holds the seam closed; the tape closes what the stitching opened.

Apply over completed sewn seams. Every sewn seam, every time. There is no waterproof PUL build without sealed seams.

"Heat-activated seam sealing tape must be applied over all sewn seams for waterproof integrity. Stitching creates needle holes through the TPU film, and seam sealing tape melts into those holes to restore the barrier. Apply tape using a warm iron or heat applicator over completed stitched seams — tape is a sealant, not a structural replacement for stitching."

No pre-washing required. The fabric ships cut-and-sew ready. Manufacturing odor from thermal bonding — if present — dissipates within 24 to 72 hours of airing,
or after a single wash-dry cycle.

08 — Care & Specifications The fabric works · if the bond holds

The fabric works
when you protect
what holds it together.

Three hundred wash cycles is the rated durability — but only if the bond between film and knit stays intact. The polyurethane film carries the entire waterproof load. The film survives normal laundering. The film does not survive what coats it, what burns through it, or what breaks the laminate bond. What follows is what to do, and what never to do. The first list is short. The second list is shorter, and absolute.

"ProSoft® Waterproof 1 mil ECO-PUL™ maintains its Waterproof 300X Technology (300+ wash cycle durability) when care protocols protect the polyurethane film from chemical degradation and heat damage. The primary threats to longevity are fabric softeners, chlorine bleach, and excessive dryer heat — each can permanently destroy the waterproof barrier."

Care 01 · Washing

Hot water is safe. Warm water lasts longer.

Hot water is rated safe to 145–165°F, though warm water extends film lifespan. Use a zero-residue liquid detergent. Add an extra rinse cycle to remove detergent residues that accumulate on and degrade the polyurethane film over time. Where construction allows, protect the film side from direct agitation — methods that shield the waterproof layer extend product life.

"Hot water is safe (rated to 145–165°F), though warm water extends film lifespan. Use zero-residue liquid detergent. Add an extra rinse cycle to remove detergent residues that accumulate on and degrade the polyurethane film over time. Protect the film side during washing when possible — construction methods that shield the waterproof layer from direct agitation extend product life."

Care 02 · Drying

Low to medium heat. Never above 130°F.

Low to medium heat only. High heat causes polyurethane breakdown that appears as stiffening, cracking, or delamination. Line drying is always safe.

Never above 130°F(50°C)

The threshold above which polyurethane breaks down. Below it, the laminate bond holds for the rated 300+ cycles.

Failure modes — Stiffening· Cracking· Delamination

"Low to medium heat only — never exceed 130°F (50°C). High heat causes polyurethane breakdown that appears as stiffening, cracking, or delamination. Line drying is always safe."
