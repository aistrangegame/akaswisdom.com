The picture behind the day

Fifteen years of the record, connected. Take what serves the films; the final calls are made together in the edit.

1Six threads through all four films

One gesture, repeated. Someone else's failure lands; the family absorbs it fast — the Hundred Days at Modi (1985–86) · Fabrite poisoning the ground (2009) · Wazoodle Hell turned around in days (2012) · mills shut by order, the network rebuilt across states (2020) · the website failing, rebuilt in four days (2021). Film 01 is the pattern; Films 02–04 are three times it happened. Every product began with someone asking. 2007 "do you have a fabric that does this?" · Megan → Ready-AbZORB · a customer's ask → Zorb 3D · emails from Asia → masks · Wazoodle was a customer before it was a purchase. Every decision connects to the person it helped — and started with that person. Three roles, held for decades. Sid makes (names a fabric by touch; credits "the environment"; no review names him). Arch says, and refuses (the no to Fabrite, "the only way is up," five times the price declined, "no tests were performed"; 360 reviews name her). Ash holds it and witnesses (the site, the Fabric Finder, the four-day rebuild; "When you tell your story backwards it almost sounds easy. But it wasn't."). Trust runs both ways. The family gives knowledge away ("we publish what things are made of"; "a discount ends; a specification does not"). The makers protect them back: Anastasia never named her vendor · Nancy V.: "don't tell anyone where I buy" · makers confronted a counterfeit-Zorb seller before the family knew · Emily: "give Arch a chance." The ground. Fabrite poisoned it; AKAS was born refusing it. Made in USA means self-sufficiency — Arch: "being at the mercy of other countries for essential supplies is the worst position." SupAbsorb catches spills before they reach soil; Film 04 closes the circle Film 01 opens. The cost was never written down. The record holds dates and deeds; the feeling is almost absent. That's what the day is for.
