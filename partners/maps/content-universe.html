Fifteen years of replies, written down once

One stroke, one piece · 687 pieces · 12 groups Taller strokes run longer · touch a group to open it

Finding the right fabric for whatever someone was making — one reply at a time, for fifteen years — left behind the deepest record in this industry of what people make, what they ask, and where cloth fails them. This is that record, turned into everything we intend to write, sorted by the person who'll read it. Here is what it is, where it came from, and what it's for.

Before the map

What this is.687 pieces, each with a title, a reader and a home — the plan for everything Wazoodle and AKAS Tex should write.

Not a library yet. A map. Twelve groups, nineteen categories of thing people make, and under them the guides, comparisons, boundary pieces and stories a person needs on the way to the right fabric. It is the foundation layer that wazoodle.com, akastex.com and help.wazoodle will render from — and, because it is written plainly and answers real questions, it is what the AI search engines will find when someone asks them what we used to be asked directly.

Where it came from.Fifteen years of replying to whoever wrote in.

The structure was never designed. It arrived one reply at a time — a customer describing what they were making, and the person who makes the cloth answering. Every email, every question, every application it was used for, every industry it went into, every function we knit for, every feature we offer and every technology we developed to answer an ask: that is the substrate. Nobody else in the chain still talks to the person who sews the fabric. That is why nobody else holds this record, and why it is unrivalled.

The one test.Is a fabric mill the right one to answer this?

Every piece passed three questions before it earned a place. Do we make a fabric that serves this? Is the piece about choosing, using or making with that fabric? Would a manufacturer be the credible author? Operations advice, procurement, facility management — however well they would serve a reader — fail regardless. And where the honest answer was we don't make that, we wrote the boundary down instead of leaving it out. The count isn't the point. The test is.

The voice.Arch's. Written to help — never to market, never to sell.

In 2020 Arch wrote one article on how to make a face mask from the fabrics on the shelf. It was written to help, and it made the pandemic story happen — five hundred orders on Friday, a thousand on Saturday, a news crew on Sunday. That has always been what worked: the blog that reads like a reply, the answer that treats the reader as capable. Every piece in this map is written in that register — relational, from inside the making — so a stranger reading at midnight hears the same voice a customer hears on the phone.

What it's for.Find the fabric. Get more ingenious. Make the dream come true.

Three altitudes, and every piece stands on one while keeping the next in view. First: the right fabric for this project, found before a wrong one is paid for. Then: what else becomes possible once the fabric is understood — the construction knowledge that used to live only in reviews, handed to the next maker. And last, only if they want it: the ladder beyond finding — swatch, yards, roll, bulk, development — walked by people who started exactly where the reader is standing. The fabric's job is to make the maker's dream come true. The map's job is to make that walk visible.

Where we lose, and where we don't.On retail yard price against imports, we lose. Apparel and Athletic answer that differently.

Apparel is import-dominated. For someone buying a few yards we compete on verified chemical-free with our own testing behind it, US manufacturing with a real address, construction that can be developed rather than selected, and a supplier who answers the phone. The larger opportunity is the brand bringing production back — they aren't comparing yard prices; they're weighing supply-chain risk, tariffs, lead time, minimums, iteration speed, and whether anyone domestic can knit it at all.

Athletic is sharper, because it can be tested. Most imported performance fabric moves moisture by an applied chemical finish that degrades with washing. ProCool moves it through fibre placement and knit structure — there is nothing to wash out. Regulation on forever chemicals is arriving on a published schedule, which turns a values conversation into a procurement one.

What this page does and doesn't claim.Where the map is thorough, where it's still thin, and how to read that.

Every piece has a title and a home. Roughly a third also carry an authored description of the reader — those rows say written for. The rest are mapped but not yet described, and the page shows that difference rather than smoothing it. Sixty-five of these are stories about real people; none will be written until the person in it has agreed. And almost none of this is published yet — the knowledge base underneath has to be written first, deliberately. What you are looking at is the plan and the reasoning behind it, not a library.

What the walk found

Three readers a product catalogue would never find.

Categories are organised around products. People aren't. These three are defined by a relationship rather than a purchase — which is why no product taxonomy had anywhere to put them, and why they only appeared once the walk followed the reader instead of the shelf.

The mother's own body

Postpartum protection, nursing pads, nursing covers — filed under baby products or period products depending on shape. Nobody searching for herself thinks of herself as being in either.

The caregiver

The same person appears across incontinence, baby, medical and household categories, buying for someone who did not choose this. Four categories, and until now no door in.

Half a yard left

Not a beginner and not a business. Someone with an offcut and a use for it. A mode of working rather than a stage of one, and it recurs everywhere.

And the places where the honest answer is: we don't make that.

heat-resistant interlining · brim stiffener · woven shirting · chlorine-resistant swim knit · load-bearing webbing

A named set of components we don't supply — structural, coated, or engineered for a durability we don't knit for. They're named in the map rather than quietly omitted. For someone sewing at home, that's a pointer to source it elsewhere. For a manufacturer at volume, it's the start of a development conversation. Undeveloped is not the same as refused.

Coming home
