Two sites. Four hundred and fifty pages. One map — where every page carries its door, its reader, its walk, its voice, its shape and its sources, and links to the record behind it.

Why this page exists

So the people building Wazoodle and AKAS Tex can see the whole of both sites at once — what exists, what is planned, what each page is for and who it's for — and open any page to the knowledge it renders, before a single screen is designed.

If you read one thing

Wazoodle sells fabric by the yard, to people who make things: six doors — Journey, Find, Shop, Grow, Learn, Family — and a handful of practical pages. AKAS Tex makes the fabric and sells it by the roll, to businesses: thirteen doors from the Dream to Contact. The two sites share one family, one standard and one map; most of what one says, the other says from the other side. Nearly half the pages are not built yet, and the map is honest about which — a page exists here only where the truth it renders is written.

A sitemap is usually a list of URLs. This one is a list of promises. Each page is placed by who walks in, what they came for, which rung of the ladder they are standing on, and whose voice should be answering — and each is tied to the knowledge it will render, with one honest mark on it: ready, or not ready. Ready means the information exists somewhere we can use — a document, a walked record, the page's own profile — whatever the format, published or not. So the build order follows the knowledge, not the other way round. Tap a door to open it. Tap a page to read its whole record. Nothing here was designed yet; it was placed.

The two sites, door by door

Twenty-one doors. Each one says who it's for before it says what's behind it.

Wazoodle's doors follow the maker's own questions — where am I, what am I making, which fabric, ready for more, teach me, who are you. AKAS Tex's follow the buyer's — who are you, what can you make, for my industry, prove it, how do we start. The counts are pages behind each door; the colour is how ready they are.

Before the tree

Two sites, one table.Both sitemaps now live in one place, with the site as a column.

Until this week Wazoodle's pages and AKAS Tex's lived in two different tables with two different vocabularies, and nothing could be read across them. Now every page of both sits in one register — 451 rows — and the same seven questions are answered on each: door, reader, rung, voice, shape, sources, phase. The pages that exist as products and old links stay where Wazoodle keeps them, counted here as one row each. One table means one build order.

Nine hundred pages, seven shapes.A product page is designed once and built 619 times.

Most of the pages on Wazoodle are one of a kind: 619 product pages, 42 fabric collections, 194 application pages, 38 fabric styles, 11 mats, 683 pieces of writing. Each kind has one written profile — who arrives, why it exists, what it says, what it must not say — and the individual pages carry only what differs. Seven profiles cover more than nine hundred pages. That is what makes the build finite.

The menu is the ladder.Journey · Find · Shop · Grow · Learn · Family.

Wazoodle's six doors are the maker's own walk, in order. Journey is the first door for the one who arrived mid-quest and doesn't yet know what to look for. Find is what are you making? Shop is which fabric? Grow is the next rung — rolls, programmes, custom, the door to AKAS Tex. Learn is every question a customer ever asked, answered. Family is who we are, told through who we served. Two of those doors — Journey and Learn — did not exist until this map found nobody had one.

One telling, two readers.Wazoodle says it from the maker's side; AKAS Tex says it from the business's.

The road from a two-dollar swatch to a fabric of your own is told on both sites — once for the maker climbing it, once for the business that arrives at the top. The same is true of the family's story, of Impact, of what is certified and tested. The map pairs those pages so the builder makes one pattern twice, and neither side ever repeats the other's depth. Where one side is already written, the other side is written to match it.

Ready, or not ready.One mark per page, derived from the record, never set by hand.

A page's knowledge is ready when the information exists somewhere usable: a document with a body, the walked lines on the record, or the page's own written profile. Published or not, verified or not, in whatever format — irrelevant. The builder doesn't need it in one shape; they need to know it exists and where. 272 of 451 pages are ready today; 179 are not, and for those nothing behind the page is written anywhere yet. The mark is a formula on the record, so it changes the moment a document gets a body. Nobody designs an empty page.

Nineteen clusters always exist. Applications earn their page.Written and wanted, or a line on the cluster page.

The Find door holds nineteen communities — baby, pet, food, medical, menstrual, and on — and every one has a page, always. Beneath them, an application gets its own page only where two things are true: its knowledge is written, and people are actually looking for it. Otherwise it is a line on its cluster page, with a redirect, until either changes. The clusters are the shelf; the applications are what has earned a place on it.

A swatch at every altitude.AKAS Tex now sells the sample too.

Wazoodle always sold the two-dollar swatch. Now AKAS Tex sells it as well — the swatch, the kits by brand, family and project, and the sorbent samples — so nobody has to be sent across the road to hold the cloth before a conversation. Yards and rolls of fabric stay on Wazoodle. Bulk and custom are, and always were, AKAS Tex's conversation. The mats are the one exception: bought by the sample and by the roll on either site, and bulk through AKAS Tex.

Every page says where its knowledge lives.Documents, the walked record, the page's own profile — in one line.

A page on this map is never a blank to be filled at design time. Each one carries a single line saying where its knowledge is and how much of it is ready — 3 of 4 documents ready · 8 walked lines · the page's own profile. The documents are the guides and articles; the walked lines are the family's own telling of the page, recorded question by question; the profile is what the page is for, in our words. Any one of the three makes a page craftable. Today every AKAS Tex page carries walked lines; no Wazoodle page does yet — that is the next pass.

What the walk found

Where both sites stand today, and what decides the order.

The build order is the knowledge order.

First, the pages whose knowledge is ready and whose door carries the room: Wazoodle's Home, Journey and the seven communities with writing behind them, the brand families and their collections, the Grow door; AKAS Tex's Home, Dream, Journey, Industries, Brands and fabric styles. Then the pages that are ready and wait — Learn, the Family door's depth, the function collections, the partner pages, and AKAS Tex's Services and Impact, whose knowledge lives in the walked record rather than in documents. Last, and only after a writing pass: the twelve communities and the applications nobody has written for yet, the humanitarian door, the crossings.

What is still open.

Three questions stay on the record rather than being guessed. The statement on where the fabric is made, which the certifications page rests on. Who the Family door's cover is for. And the period behind the demand numbers that rank the applications. Each is marked on its row; none blocks the pages ready today.

For our side

One register holds both sites, with a column for the site and a page for every kind. Each row carries the placement rule that set it — reader and voice by door and page type, rung by what the page sells, phase by whether its knowledge is written and how much it is asked for — and a note where a ruling changed it. The profiles for the pages that are one of a kind, and for the doors, were written once each and are being carried into the rows now. AKAS Tex's wings — Industries, Technologies, Brands, the fabric styles — are assembled from the record in order of readiness and verified before they lock.

Coming home

You've walked both sites. Here's what they're saying.

A site is a set of promises, not a set of URLs.

Every page here was placed by who walks in and what they came for before anyone asked what it should look like. The reader, the rung, the voice and the sources came first; the design comes from those. That order is the whole method, and it is why four hundred and fifty pages can be built by a small team without a single one being invented at the keyboard.

The two sites are one conversation heard from two chairs.

Wazoodle hears the maker; AKAS Tex hears the business. The road between them — swatch, yards, roll, the crossing, bulk, development — is the same road told twice, and the map keeps the two tellings honest with each other. Nobody is pushed across. The next door is visible, and that is all.

The page follows the document.

Half the map is not built, and the map says so plainly. A page waits until the truth it renders is written — a fabric's guide, a community's article, a family answer to a question nobody has asked them yet. Building in that order means nothing on either site will ever say something we cannot stand behind.

What was ready before we were.

Most of the knowledge these pages need already exists — two hundred and twenty-six documents written, seven hundred pieces planned, thirteen films mapped, nine kinds of customer named, five voices tuned. The sitemap didn't create the sites. It found them, already standing, in the record of fifteen years of answering the phone.

So the question that opens the build is the one that opens both sites: who is this page for, and what is it for?

The pages were placed before they were drawn. The walk was ready before we were.
