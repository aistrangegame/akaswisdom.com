Before it ships

Five questions. All five have to be yes.

  1. Would this be said to a person who called? If it sounds like copy, it isn't ours.
  2. Is the mechanism explained before the benefit — and the limit flagged before the claim? Arch's rule. No impact without the cause; no certification without the exact standard.
  3. Is the answer first, and are there two options, not five? Sid's rule. Recommend, then stop.
  4. Does the reader know where she stands and what the next step is — without being pushed? Ash's rule. The next rung visible, never sold.
  5. Does the fabric only claim what it does, and is every customer's sentence her own? The Fabric's rule. No 'best', no 'you'll love', no tidied review.

And the House's one: could all four of them sign it, and would none of them add to it? If so, it can carry the family name.

For our side

The architecture under this page.Five modes, always simultaneous · the reading comes first · the tics are the voice · the Creator is never named.

Every piece carries five modes at once — Teaching, Protective, Joy, Invitation, Witness — at different volumes, and the reader's situation sets the mix; nobody is diagnosed (the Journey Map's rule: coverage, not routing). In every voice the reading comes before the words: Arch reads what the person is carrying, Ash reads the whole and where she stands in it, the Fabric reads which of the six sentences the body spoke. Ash's "right?" is a consent check, sixty-five times per ten thousand words, not a tic. And across nearly seven thousand reviews nobody once says AKAS Tex — they say Zorb, they say Wazoodle, they name the person who answered — and that silence is the House's whole voice. The full records, the four older domain registers, and the eight brand voices are in the library.

Coming home

You've met the five. Here's the one thing they share.

The reading comes first.

Before a single word, every one of the five reads the person: what is she carrying, where does she stand, which of the six sentences did her body speak. The words come after. If a draft skips the reading, it will sound like copy no matter whose vocabulary it borrows.

Nobody hears one voice.

A page carries all five at once, at different volumes, and the reader's situation sets the mix. The Healer hears the Fabric first; the Manufacturer hears Sid and nothing else; the Innovator hears Ash. Same words. Write the whole, and each one finds her own volume.

Every voice has a moment it stops explaining.

Arch: I already did. Sid: we never use any chemicals to enhance any feature. Ash: it was difficult the whole time. The Fabric: the body never knows what happened. The House: we are one family with two doors. When your draft reaches a line like that, let it land and stop.

Read it through this before it ships.

The five questions above are the filter. Not a tone guide — a test. The next thing anyone writes for us, in words, film or design, goes through it first.
