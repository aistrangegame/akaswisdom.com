The Knowledge Map

Everything we know, written down once, so every surface can say the same thing.

Why this page exists

Fifteen years of answering one question at a time left an enormous amount of knowledge in one family’s heads, in old emails, and in replies nobody kept. This is the effort to write it down once, in one place, in a neutral voice — so a product page, a help article, a landing page, a film brief and an agency deck can all render from the same source instead of each inventing its own version.

Where you’ll be when you’ve read it

You’ll know what the knowledge base holds, what it doesn’t, and which parts are trustworthy enough to build on today. You’ll be able to open any document yourself. And you’ll know which gap we’d fill first, and why that one.

What this is, exactly

One source, eight layers

423 documents. 226 of them written; the rest exist as named, placed, empty records — the roadmap rather than the library. 160 are already live and public. Every single document reaches at least one page on one of the two websites, so nothing here is knowledge with nowhere to go.

Neutral register, on purpose. These documents teach; they do not sell. Precision everywhere — weights in GSM, stretch percentages, absorption ratios, wash-cycle counts — and honest trade-offs given equal weight to strengths. Voice is added later, per surface. The canonical layer stays plain so that everything downstream can borrow from it without inheriting a tone.

What is not here. Nothing about how the cloth is made — no process, no supplier, no method. That is a permanent boundary, not a gap. And 47 documents are flagged for verification, almost all of them in the fabric layer; they are marked, not hidden.

What the shape says

We wrote about the cloth

Read the two ends of the table against each other and the whole knowledge base says one thing.

The layers describing the material — the fabrics, the functions, the technologies, the construction families — are 165 documents, 98% written. Complete, precise, and among the best of their kind anywhere in this industry.

The layer describing what a person actually makes is 194 documents, 14% written.

And the customers have done the exact opposite. Across those same layers they have sent us 938 photographs of things they made — and not one of them is of a fabric. Sixty-one of those pages now hold a customer’s picture of the finished thing and not a single word of our own about how to make it.

A mill writes about cloth because that is what a mill knows. But nobody searches for a fabric. They search for the thing they are trying to make.

What gets written next

The queue, in the order the evidence gives it

Four bodies of work, and they are not equally hard. Two of them are mostly assembly from material we already hold.

One · the 165 unwritten applications

The largest and the most valuable. Each one is a person’s project, and the fabric knowledge behind it is already written — the work is to turn what the material does into what the maker needs to do. Sixty-one already have a customer photograph waiting on the page. Those are the ones to write first: the evidence arrived before the article.

Two · the three empty layers

Services (0 of 10), Humanitarian (0 of 3) and Crossings (1 of 9). Small, and entirely absent. These are the manufacturer’s side and the ladder between the two doors — the knowledge that lives in the family’s heads and has never been written anywhere.

Three · the 47 flagged documents

Almost all in the brand fabric layer — ProSoft 10, ProECO 9, ProCool 7, Zorb 6. Written, useful, and carrying product citations that need re-checking against the live catalogue before anyone builds a page on them. This is verification, not authorship.

Four · the industries

15 of 23 written, and the eight missing ones are the markets nobody has documented — the same ones where no customer picture exists either. The thinnest evidence in the whole system sits here, which makes it the place to be most careful about what we claim.

Coming home

Build the source once, render it everywhere

This is not a content library and it is not a website. It is the layer underneath both. A product page, a help article, a landing page, a film brief and a printed package are five renderings of the same document — and when the document is corrected, all five inherit the correction.

That is the whole argument for writing it down once. It is also why the holes on this page are counted rather than hidden: an unwritten document is a page that five surfaces will each improvise separately.
