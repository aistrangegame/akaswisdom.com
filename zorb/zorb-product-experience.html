The Fabric Journey
Six frames. From where you are to what this fabric is.
Before · What you've tried
Five materials. Each solving one problem while creating another.
Microfiber — compression leaks. Hemp — absorbent, but slow. Flour sack towels — bulk without capacity. Bamboo inserts — better, but still needed four layers to get through the night.
You kept looking because the problem wasn't effort. It was the material.
Face 1 · Origin
Born from the loom, not the lab.
Zorb begins as four fibers — cotton, polyester, viscose from bamboo, and nylon — spun together in a single needlefelt pass. No chemical bonding. No adhesives. The structure IS the technology.
"You have never been on the surface."
Face 2 · Absorption
Ten times its weight. Under two seconds.
Moisture doesn't sit on the surface — it's pulled into the fiber matrix by capillary action. Wicking begins on contact. No SAP. No super-absorbent polymers. The absorption is the fiber itself.
"What enters does not return."
Face 3 · Structure
0.080 inches thick. 235 grams per square meter.
Thinner than a pencil eraser. The stiffness you feel on first touch is the fiber structure in its pre-wash state. That stiffness becomes your cutting advantage. After enclosure and first wash — transformation.
"The stiffness is temporary. The softness is permanent."
Face 4 · Compression
Zero compression bleed.
When you press a Zorb layer under body weight, fluid stays held. No squeeze-out. No lateral spread. This is what makes the overnight possible — pressure doesn't release what the fiber captured.
"Hold is not the absence of release. Hold is architecture."
Face 5 · Endurance
Hundreds of washes. The fiber holds.
Needlefelt doesn't unravel — there are no woven threads to come loose. The structure that absorbs on day one absorbs on wash three hundred. This is engineered permanence.
"The first wash transforms. Every wash after confirms."
