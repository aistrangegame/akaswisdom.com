02 — What It Is Zorb Original · AKAS Wisdom

Hidden.
Thin.

These two facts are the whole engineering story.

Zorb Original is not a surface fabric. It lives inside your work — enclosed between layers, never touching skin, always absorbing. Two facts define it: it disappears into the build, and it performs more than its thickness suggests.

Other Zorb fabrics — the 3D Dimple families — have finished faces and can sit against skin as the surface layer. Zorb Original cannot. It's the working layer that lives inside the build, always enclosed between two other fabrics. If your project needs a stay-dry surface that's also the Zorb, look at the 3D Dimple variants instead.

Zorb is what's in the middle — the layer doing the work that the outer layers get credit for.

Technology · 01 of 02

Hydro-Dual Fiber.

Two fiber types working in concert. One captures liquid immediately on contact. The other distributes it through the structure in three dimensions.

Liquid arrives at one point and spreads through the whole layer — rather than pooling. The fabric absorbs as a system, not as a sheet.

Capture is fast. Distribution is what makes it stay.

Station 01 · Arrive & Inspect

It arrives stiff.
That's not a defect.

Zorb Original is stiff from the bolt — tightly structured fibers ready to absorb but not yet integrated. The stiffness is intentional. After the first wash the fabric softens and develops the pillow-like texture it's known for.

The stiffness also makes initial cutting easier — it holds shape as a template for cutting your other layers.

Why
The tight manufacturing structure improves absorption performance. It resolves through washing, not handling. Don't condition before you work with it.

Station 02 · Cut

Scissors over
rotary cutter.

Sharp scissors give cleaner edges on Zorb's layered fiber structure than a rotary cutter. Trace your pattern with permanent marker directly on the fabric.

Cut Zorb first — while stiff, it holds shape precisely as a template for your other layers. Shrinkage is minimal (1–3%), so accurate pattern cutting is straightforward.

Why
The rotary cutter can catch on Zorb's layered structure. Scissors follow fiber direction cleanly. Cutting Zorb first while stiff saves time aligning your full stack.

Station 03 · Needle

Match needle
to layer count.

For single-layer Zorb applications, a universal needle works well. For multiple Zorb layers, switch to an embroidery needle — its larger eye and batting-appropriate point geometry penetrates the dense layered structure more cleanly.

The functional difference is in the point shape and eye size, not just height.

Why
Multiple Zorb layers behave like batting. The embroidery needle's larger eye feeds thread more consistently through the density without catching on the fiber structure.

Station 04 · Machine Setup

Too fast →
it stretches.

Zorb can stretch slightly if pushed through the machine too quickly. Sew at moderate speed and use an even-feed (walking) foot.

The walking foot feeds all layers evenly without shifting — it moves top and bottom layers simultaneously so the presser foot doesn't drag the top layer ahead of the Zorb underneath. That's the mechanism that prevents stretch.

Why
Without a walking foot, the presser foot can push the top fabric faster than the Zorb moves — creating slight stretch at the seam. The walking foot eliminates this entirely.

Station 05 · Layering

Each layer
slightly smaller.

When stacking multiple Zorb layers, make each layer approximately one inch smaller than the layer below — this graduated sizing prevents bulk at seam edges.

Always place a natural fiber layer between multiple Zorb layers — cotton birdseye works well — to help the stack compress cleanly. Bar-tack the layers together before final assembly to prevent shifting during use and washing.

Why
Cotton between layers helps Zorb compress and release correctly. Bar-tacking holds the layered stack registered through laundering — absorption stays even, the geometry doesn't drift.

before the first wash.

Zorb must be enclosed between two fabric layers before its first washing. The outer layers protect Zorb's fiber structure through laundering.

Un-enclosed Zorb pills, sheds, and loses structural integrity permanently — there is no recovery from this. The sequence is fixed: sew it in, then pre-wash for hygiene before body contact.

Station 07 · Edge Finishing

It won't fray
aggressively.

Zorb Original doesn't fray aggressively — for most applications, raw edges enclosed within a product are fine.

For projects involving heavy laundering or high-stress seams, finish exposed edges with a serger or zigzag stitch. This is a finishing choice that adds durability, not a structural requirement that prevents failure.

Why
The layered fiber structure holds without finishing. Heavier laundering cycles are where finishing adds meaningful durability — seam stress accumulates across many washes.

08 — Care & Specs The rules · the numbers · the close

The rules.
The numbers.

Four care rules. One is a hard line — fabric softener kills absorption permanently. The other three are the rhythm Zorb settles into.

Rule 01 · The line that cannot be crossed

Never fabric softener.

Fabric softener coats fibers with a hydrophobic film. That film blocks the very mechanism that makes Zorb absorb. Once applied, the coating bonds at the fiber level — washing won't remove it. The fabric is structurally sound but functionally finished.

This applies to the entire stack — not just the Zorb layer. Softener anywhere in the wash cycle will reach the Zorb through the fabric stack.

!
This cannot be undone. No wash cycle, soak, or strip removes fabric softener once it has bonded. If softener has touched your Zorb, the fabric is no longer absorbent. Replace it. There is no recovery path.
Avoid: Liquid fabric softener · dryer sheets · scent boosters with softening agents · "spring fresh" detergents with built-in softeners
Rule 02 · Wash

Hot water.
Zero residue.

Wash hot — hot water clears soap residue from the fiber structure between cycles. Use a fragrance-free, residue-free detergent like Tide Free & Gentle or All Free Clear. Fragrance and dye additives can build up over time and gradually reduce absorption.

Oxygen-based bleach is acceptable for both variants — Tide Oxi, OxiClean, color-safe bleach. Chlorine bleach is not. For W-202 Standard chlorine degrades fiber integrity; for W-201 Silver it permanently destroys the Silver Plus antimicrobial treatment.

Wash: Hot · fragrance-free detergent · oxygen bleach OK · chlorine bleach never
Rule 03 · Dry

Medium heat.
Gets better.

Tumble dry medium heat. Zorb softens with use — the stiff structure from the bolt resolves into the pillow-like texture customers describe over the first 5–10 wash-and-dry cycles. The fabric improves through the rhythm rather than wearing out.

Air drying works but is slower. Heat speeds the structural integration of the layered fibers without damaging them.

Dry: Medium heat · tumble dry · air dry acceptable · 5–10 cycles to reach finished texture
Rule 04 · Pre-wash

Sequence
matters.

Zorb is pre-processed for absorption — it does not require pre-washing for performance. But for any project that touches skin, pre-wash for hygiene before first body contact.

The sequence is fixed: sew it in first, then pre-wash. Un-enclosed Zorb damages permanently in the wash. After the fabric is sealed between two layers, hygiene pre-washing is normal and recommended.

Sequence: Sew Zorb between layers · then pre-wash for hygiene · then use
