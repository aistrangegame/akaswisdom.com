Here's what you're facing

Here's what happens after a slip-and-fall in a warehouse. An OSHA recordable incident goes on your record. Your experience modification rate — the number your insurance company uses to calculate your premiums — goes up. And it stays up for three years.

That's not a typo. One incident on a Tuesday afternoon affects what you pay every month for the next 36 months. Slip-and-fall claims cost employers $40,000 to $48,000 on average. But the real cost is the mod rate — the compounding premium increase that keeps going long after the floor's been mopped.

And right now, there's something most warehouse managers don't know about.

In 2024, OSHA launched a National Emphasis Program targeting warehouses and distribution centers specifically. A directive telling compliance officers: go inspect these facilities. The program runs through at least 2027 and focuses on walking-working surfaces, powered vehicles, and material handling. OSHA isn't waiting for complaints. They're showing up.

OSHA cites each violation separately. At over $16,000 per serious violation, the math adds up fast.

Here's the part that surprises most people.

29 CFR 1910.22(a)(2)

There's a federal regulation that says warehouse floors must be kept dry. And where wet conditions exist, the regulation says you have to provide "dry standing places, such as false floors, platforms, and mats."

Not signs. Not cones. The regulation specifically names mats as one of the compliance mechanisms. If your floor is wet and you don't have absorbent mats down, you're not just at risk — you're already out of compliance.
